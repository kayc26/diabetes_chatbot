Why Blood Glucose 
Management
 
Matters
Insulin keeps you healthy but balancing blood glucose matters too.
High blood glucose (hyperglycemia) over time may affect blood vessels and blood flow which leads to issues with 
your eyes, kidneys, heart, and nerves. Low blood glucose (hypoglycemia) also happens often for people living with 
type 1 diabetes. 
Managing Low Blood Glucose (Hypoglycemia)
Hypoglycemia can happen when your blood glucose drops below 70 mg/dL as a result of insulin, food 
(especially
 
carbohydrates), and physical activity being out of balance.
Some of the reasons hypoglycemia may occur:
You may notice common early signs and symptoms, such as shakiness, 
sweating, hunger, dizziness, confusion, irritability, weakness, or fatigue. 
These vary from person to person and may develop quickly, which is why 
it's important to know when to address them.
Severe Hypoglycemia
Severe hypoglycemia can happen when blood

Treating Hypoglycemia: The 15-15 Rule
When you feel symptoms of hypoglycemia, check your blood glucose level as soon as you can, then treat it right 
away. Ask your healthcare team what they recommend if you ever have the symptoms of low blood glucose but 
cannot check your level right away. Then you know in advance how to stay as safe as possible.
Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) systems read glucose levels from the fluid between your body’s cells, 
called interstitial fluid, and not directly from your blood. CGM readings may lag behind Blood Glucose Monitoring 
(BGM) readings by 5
 
minutes or more. When you’re feeling low, or your levels are changing quickly, use a BGM 
device to get the most current, real-time number for treatment and to verify your blood glucose level.
The 15-15 rule is a simple way to remember how to treat hypoglycemia with fast-acting carbohydrates. 
Fast-
acting carbohydrates enter your bloodstream fast and raise your blood glucose level quickly.

Emergency Treatment for 
Severe
 
Hypoglycemia
When blood glucose falls dangerously low, or you can’t treat it by yourself, action is needed right away 
to prevent serious harm. Repeated severe lows can affect brain function over time, so prevention and 
preparation
 
matter.
If you are too confused, vomiting, or unable to safely eat or drink, 
glucagon comes to the rescue. It’s a lifesaving prescription medicine that 
quickly raises blood glucose when you need it most.
Today, many glucagon products are available in ready-to-use forms, 
making them easier for family members or caregivers to administer during 
an emergency. Talk with your healthcare team about choosing a glucagon 
option that is simple to use when needed.
