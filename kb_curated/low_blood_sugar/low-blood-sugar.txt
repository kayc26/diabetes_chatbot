Low blood glucose (hypoglycemia)

Low and high blood glucose are common short-term issues in type 2 diabetes. They can
happen due to your medications, what you eat, or changes in your physical activity.

What is low blood glucose (hypoglycemia)?

Low blood glucose is when your blood glucose is too low, usually below 70 mg/dL.

A few of the common causes of low blood glucose are:
- Taking too much insulin or other diabetes medications
- Too little food, or meals are delayed
- More physical activity than usual (this can last several hours after being active)

Common symptoms:
- Shakiness
- Sweating
- Hunger
- Dizziness
- Confusion
- Feeling weak

How should I treat low blood glucose?

To treat low blood glucose (under 70 mg/dL) use the Rule of 15:
1. Eat or drink 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrates like:
   - Glucose tablets or gel
   - 1/2 cup (4 ounces) of juice or regular soda
   - 1 tablespoon of sugar or honey
2. Recheck your blood glucose after 15 minutes.
3. If your blood glucose is still low, repeat.

Once your blood glucose is above 70 mg/dL or trending up on your continuous glucose
monitor (CGM), eat a protein and carbohydrate snack (example: cheese and crackers) if
your next meal is more than an hour away.

If you are at risk for low blood glucose, always carry a quick source of carbs, like
glucose tablets or gels, in case your blood glucose drops too low. Talk to your health
care team about how to prevent and treat low blood glucose. Also ask if you are at risk
for severe hypoglycemia and need a prescription for glucagon.

How can I prevent low blood glucose?
- Take medications as prescribed. Do not take more than prescribed.
- Follow your eating plan for meals and snacks.
- Check your blood glucose regularly, especially when changing your routine.
- Adjust food or medication when you are physically active (talk to your health care
  team about how).
- Carry a fast-acting carbohydrate with you at all times.

Review the results of your blood glucose monitoring and any patterns you see with your
health care team to make sure you do not need to adjust your treatment plan to avoid low
blood glucose.
