Ketones and Diabetic Ketoacidosis
If your body doesn’t have enough insulin to use blood glucose for 
energy, it begins using your body fat instead. This process creates 
ketones that can build up in your blood and show up in your urine 
as your body is working to get rid of them. When ketones get too 
high, they can cause a life-threatening condition called diabetic 
ketoacidosis (DKA) that needs immediate medical care.
You can check ketones using urine test strips (20 mg/dL and higher 
warrants attention) or a blood ketone meter (0.6 mmol/L and higher 
warrants attention). You and your healthcare team will develop an 
action plan for when to check for ketones  and what to do if your 
ketone results are high.
You are more likely to develop ketones when:
• Y
our blood glucose levels are above >200 mg/dL
• Y
ou have less insulin in your body, which is 
commonly due to a problem with your insulin 
delivery device or missing an insulin dose
• Y
ou’re sick, vomiting, dehydrated, or under stress
There is is a condition called euglycemic DKA that 
can happen even when your blood glucose is not very 
high, especially if you don't eat enough carbs, take 
SGLT-2 inhibitors, use an insulin pump, or are sick.
