What is type 1 diabetes?
In type 1 diabetes, the body's immune system mistakenly attacks the insulin-producing beta cells in the 
pancreas. It recognizes these cells as foreign invaders and gradually damages them, resulting in little or no 
insulin production.
When you eat, your body breaks food down into glucose and sends it to your cells. When glucose enters your 
cells, it is either used as fuel for energy right away or stored for later use. Without insulin, glucose builds up in 
the blood instead of fueling the body. That’s why you need insulin every day to replace what your body can no 
longer make. Your goal is to keep your blood glucose within your target range so your body can function well 
and stay healthy.
Keeping your blood glucose in your target range takes a treatment plan and a little detective work to watch 
your patterns. Over time, you’ll start to notice how your blood glucose responds to things like food and physical 
activity from day to day—and you’ll learn how to make adjustments. Your healthcare team is there to help you 
figure out what works best for your needs and goals.
The goal isn’t to have perfect blood glucose levels; it’s to understand and take steps to help you stay in your 
target range. Understanding how food, physical activity, stress, and illness affect your glucose will help you get 
better at spotting your own patterns over time and the steps you can make to stay in your target range.
