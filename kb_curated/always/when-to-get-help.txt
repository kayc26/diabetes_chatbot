When to get help right away

If your blood glucose levels stay high or you start feeling symptoms like nausea,
vomiting, or confusion, contact your doctor right away. These could be signs of
diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), a serious complication of diabetes that occurs when your
body produces a high amount of ketones due to a lack of insulin.

Low blood glucose is when your blood glucose is too low, usually below 70 mg/dL. To
treat low blood glucose use the Rule of 15. If your blood glucose is still low after
you repeat it, contact your health care team.

If you are at risk for low blood glucose, always carry a quick source of carbs, like
glucose tablets or gels, in case your blood glucose drops too low. Talk to your health
care team about how to prevent and treat low blood glucose. Also ask if you are at risk
for severe hypoglycemia and need a prescription for glucagon.

When you are sick

Preparing in advance and knowing how to care for yourself when you are sick can help
you manage your food, fluids, and medications. When you are sick, blood glucose levels
can rise, even if you are eating less.

- Check your blood glucose at least every four hours or as suggested by your health
  care team.
- Keep taking your diabetes medications, including insulin. You may need to adjust the
  dose based on your blood glucose results.
- Drink plenty of fluids to prevent dehydration. If you are unable to eat, you may need
  to replace your usual meals with carbohydrate-containing fluids.

Keeping your blood glucose in your target range helps prevent both short- and long-term
health problems. Highs and lows can happen with changes in your living situation, food,
activity, or medications, so regular monitoring and support from your health care team
will help you stay healthy.
