Source: Team FAQs

Q: I am still hungry between meals. How do I fix that?
A: Hunger between meals may mean your meals are not balanced enough. Try including a source of protein (such as eggs, chicken, fish, tofu, Greek yogurt, or beans), fiber (vegetables, fruit, whole grains, or beans), and healthy fats (nuts, seeds, avocado, or olive oil) at meals. Staying hydrated and eating on a regular schedule can also help control hunger.

Q: How many carbohydrates should I eat per day?
A: Carbohydrate needs vary based on age, weight, activity level, medications, and health goals. Many adults with diabetes aim for about 30-60 grams of carbohydrates per meal, but there is no one-size-fits-all recommendation.

Q: How can I increase my protein intake?
A: Add lean protein foods to meals and snacks, such as eggs, chicken, turkey, fish, tofu, tempeh, beans, lentils, edamame, cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, nuts, seeds, or protein shakes. Including protein at each meal may help with fullness, blood sugar management, and maintaining muscle mass.

Q: What beverages can I drink?
A: Water is the best choice. Other options include unsweetened tea, black coffee, sparkling water, flavored water with no added sugar, and low-fat milk. Limit sugar-sweetened beverages such as regular soda, sweet tea, sports drinks, juice, and energy drinks.

Q: What fruit can I eat?
A: Fruit can fit into a healthy meal plan but it's a good idea to keep the portion to 1 cup or 1 small sized fruit. Berries, peaches, pears, plums, cherries, and apples tend to have less of an effect on blood sugar than fruits like melon and bananas. Consider pairing fruit with a protein source such as nuts, cheese, peanut butter, or yogurt to help improve fullness and reduce large blood sugar spikes.

Q: Food is expensive. What can I eat that is healthy?
A: Many nutritious foods are affordable. Consider beans, lentils, eggs, peanut butter, canned tuna, frozen vegetables, frozen fruit, oatmeal, brown rice, potatoes, and store-brand products. Planning meals, buying foods on sale, and choosing frozen produce can help stretch your budget.

Q: What protein shakes can I have?
A: Look for shakes that provide protein with limited added sugar. Compare labels and choose options with at least 20 grams of protein and lower amounts of added sugar (<5 grams) and total carbohydrate (<20 grams).

Q: What snacks can I have with diabetes?
A: Good snack options often combine protein and about 15 grams of complex carbohydrate. Examples include:
  - Apple with peanut butter
  - Greek yogurt and 1/2 cup berries
  - Cheese and whole-grain crackers
  - Hard-boiled eggs with 1 small fruit
  - Hummus and vegetables
  - Cottage cheese and berries
  - Roasted chickpeas
  - Mixed nuts and a small piece of fruit

Q: Do I need a bedtime snack?
A: Not everyone needs a bedtime snack. It may be beneficial if you experience low blood sugars overnight, take certain diabetes medications, exercise in the evening, or go long periods without eating.

Q: Are there supplements I can take to lower my blood sugar?
A: No supplement has been proven to replace healthy eating, physical activity, and prescribed diabetes medications. Some supplements are marketed for blood sugar control, but evidence is often limited and they may interact with medications. Always discuss supplements with your healthcare provider before starting them.
