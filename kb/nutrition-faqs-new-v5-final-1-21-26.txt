What are quality carbs I can choose?
Choose nutrient-rich—aim for 2.5g fiber or more per serving—and minimally processed carbs for your meal plan.  
Foods that are higher in carbs include starchy vegetables, beans, fruits, whole grains and low-fat dairy. These  
foods have the largest effect on blood glucose (blood sugar). Here are some examples of quality carbs: 
� Starchy vegetables: potatoes, sweet potatoes, corn, and winter squash 
� Beans: kidney, black, pinto, chickpeas, and lentils 
� Fruits: fresh, frozen, dried, or canned in fruit juice 
� Dairy: low-fat milk, yogurt, and plant-based milk (e.g., soy milk)
� Whole grains: quinoa, oats, brown rice, and whole grain products (pastas, bread, 
   and tortillas).
Do I need to think about the amount of fat in 
the foods I eat? 
�  Focus on choosing the right types of fats and keep   
    portions small. 
�  Monounsaturated fats: Found in olive oil, avocados,   
    nuts, and seeds help lower "bad" LDL cholesterol       
    while maintaining "good" HDL. 
�  Polyunsaturated fats: Found in fatty fish, flaxseed,    
    walnuts, and sunflower oil, contain omega-3 and         
    omega-6 fatty acids, which support brain and heart   
    health. 
�  Limit or avoid saturated fats: Found in butter, red      
    meat, full-fat dairy, and coconut oil. These can raise   
    LDL cholesterol and increase the risk of heart        
    disease if you eat too much of them.
Who on my diabetes care team 
do I talk to about nutrition? 
Anyone on your diabetes care team 
can answer general questions, but it's 
best to talk to a registered dietitian 
nutritionist (RDN) about nutrition and 
your eating plan.
Is there one diet people with diabetes            
should follow? 
There isn't one single “diet” for managing diabetes—there 
are many types of eating patterns people can follow.   
         
One place to start is the Diabetes Plate:
�  Fill half your 9” plate with non-starchy vegetables
•  Fill 1/4 with lean protein foods
�  Fill 1/4 with a quality carbohydrate    
 (starchy vegetables, beans, fruits,     
 whole  grains, low fat milk or yogurt)
�  Choose a low-calorie drink, like     
 water, unsweetened tea, or coffee 
Talk to your RDN to create an eating    
plan to meet your needs and to help     
you reach your health goals.
Do I need to lower the amount of sodium in 
my eating plan? 
�  Limit the amount of sodium you eat to less than      
    2,300 milligrams per day. 
�  Most of the sodium we eat comes from highly      
    processed food, so try to eat mostly whole foods.  
�  When eating packaged or canned foods, look for      
   “low sodium” or “no-added-salt” or minimally            
    processed on their labels.  
�  Low sodium packaged or canned foods contain      
    140mg of sodium or less per serving.
   If you’re taking insulin, you should work with an RDN or certified diabetes care and education specialist 
(CDCES) to be sure your eating plan has the carbs needed for your insulin plan.
